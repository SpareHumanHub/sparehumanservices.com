Dog Walking & Pet Sitting in Redington Beach

Professional, licensed, and insured pet care serving Redington Beach and nearby communities in Pinellas County — your local pet sitter near me for the barrier islands. GPS-tracked walks, photo updates, and reliable scheduling — whether you're a year-round resident or visiting for the season.

Redington Beach is a barrier island town with a few quiet residential blocks and not much else — no real commercial strip, one road in and out along Gulf Boulevard, and a year-round population that swells every winter. It sits between Redington Shores to the north and North Redington Beach to the south, and it's one of the calmest walking territories I cover. If your dog does best with predictable, low-stimulation routes, this is about as good as island living gets.

Walks here stick to the residential grid — calm side streets, bay-side glimpses between the houses, and shaded pockets where the older Florida landscaping still stands. Just north, Indian Rocks Beach adds the nature preserve trails and Bicentennial Park to the route options, and I use both for clients whose dogs want a change of scenery without a car ride across the county.

Walks built for the island

Redington Beach sits in Pinellas County's first-wave hurricane evacuation zone, and I plan around that reality with every client here. When a storm threatens, I help with early pet prep — carriers staged, meds documented, evacuation kit current — and I point everyone to my pet hurricane prep guide before June 1 each year. Nobody wants to be building an evacuation plan for their dog at 2 AM under a hurricane warning.

What to know about dogs in Redington Beach

The Gulf beaches here don't allow dogs — Redington Shores posts it plainly, and the barrier island towns hold to it — so dog exercise happens on the streets and the bay side, not the sand. That's the trade that makes the territory: almost no traffic, no crowds, and no surprises, which is exactly what reactive dogs, senior dogs, and anxious rescues need. Leash rules are county standard: six feet or shorter, waste bagged, ID tags on.

The snowbird calendar runs this island. November through April, I'm doing midday drop-ins for winter residents at lunch or out on the water while their pets hold the couch down, plus full vacation coverage for trips back north. Summer flips the script — long-term rentals thin out, the year-round locals stay, and standing weekly schedules are easiest to lock in before October.

Island logistics matter here more than anywhere else I serve. There's one causeway in and out, so visit windows are planned around bridge traffic, and parking along Gulf Boulevard disappears entirely on peak weekends — one more reason steady, scheduled visits beat last-minute scrambles. For island clients without a car at hand, my pet taxi covers vet and grooming runs to the mainland, and for longer coverage the overnight service ($100 flat, 10pm–6am) means your pet isn't alone through a stormy night or a late flight home.

Neighbor pages: Indian Rocks Beach just to the north, the rest of the island chain south through Treasure Island, and my Pinellas dog-friendly guide for the spots where dogs actually are welcome.
