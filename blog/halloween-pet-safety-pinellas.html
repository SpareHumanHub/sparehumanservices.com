Halloween Pet Safety in Pinellas County

Candy bowls, doorbell marathons, and a neighborhood full of masks — October 31 is the one night a year that turns your street into an obstacle course for your pets.

One of the dogs on my route here in Largo is named Spooky. That's just her name — nothing to do with Halloween — but it does mean I think about this holiday more than your average pet sitter. Because for the four-legged members of the house, Halloween is the night the whole neighborhood changes. The doorbell goes off every ninety seconds. Strangers in weird outfits show up at the end of the driveway after dark. And there's a bowl of candy sitting by the front door that would put a forty-pound dog in the emergency room if it got the chance.

This year Halloween falls on a Saturday, so expect bigger crowds and later nights than usual around Pinellas. Trick-or-treating in most Largo, Clearwater, and Seminole neighborhoods runs from around 6 to 8 pm, right at sunset. Here's what I do for my clients on October 31, and what I'd tell you to do at your own house.

Put the candy bowl somewhere smart

Chocolate is the number one reason people call Pet Poison Helpline every single year, and the busiest stretch of those calls starts on Halloween night. The ASPCA's poison control center fielded more than 451,000 calls in 2024 alone. The darker the chocolate, the worse it is — baking chocolate and dark chocolate are the ones that scare vets, because the toxic compound is concentrated.

But the candy that worries me more than chocolate is sugar-free gum. It's usually sweetened with xylitol, and while that's harmless for people, it can send a dog's blood sugar crashing and damage the liver. Raisins show up in those "healthy" candy mixes and are their own problem. And glow sticks — cats in particular like to chew them. The liquid is bitter, so you'll see drooling and pawing at the mouth. It's not truly toxic, but call the helpline anyway, because it's miserable for the cat and you'll want to know what to watch for.

So: keep the bowl on top of the fridge, not on a table the cat can jump on or near the door where a leash can reach it. And don't hand out candy from a bowl on the porch where your dog hangs out with you. One hundred door openings is plenty of temptation for one night.

If your dog does get into candy and you're not sure how much or what kind, don't wait for symptoms. Call Pet Poison Helpline at (855) 764-7661 or the ASPCA at (888) 426-4435. Both are staffed 24/7, including on Halloween.

The front door is where dogs get lost

I've heard some version of the same story more times than I can count: the doorbell rings, the dog beats everyone to the door, and in the two seconds between "trick or treat" and the fistful of candy, the dog is three houses down the street. Trick-or-treat hours are escape hours. Every October I adjust how I handle the dogs on my schedule that day, because the front door becomes the riskiest spot in the house.

Emmet, one of my regulars, keeping the yard secure. Photo: Spare Human Services

Pick your system before the 31st: a baby gate across the entryway, a leash on before the door opens, or the easiest one — set your dog up in a back room with something better to do, like a frozen Kong or a long-lasting chew. Thirty minutes of occupied chewing beats an hour of door-sprinting.

Two things worth doing this week, before things get busy. First, check that the tag on the collar has your current phone number — I see faded, out-of-date tags on dogs all the time. Second, if your pet is microchipped, pull up the registry and make sure the contact info is current. It takes five minutes and it's the difference between a lost dog coming home that night or not.

If a dog does slip out on Halloween, found dogs in our area end up at Pinellas County Animal Services over on Ulmerton Road, right here in Largo — their number is (727) 582-2600. File a lost report with them early and check in person the next morning. And if you find a lost dog out trick-or-treating, that's where it goes.

Take the walk early

I shift every Halloween walk earlier in the day, and I'd tell you to do the same. There are three reasons. First, the sidewalks on the 31st are littered with candy wrappers, and a wrapper that smells like chocolate is irresistible to a dog on a walk. Second, kids in masks are confusing and honestly a little scary for dogs — dogs read faces to figure out who they're meeting, and a mask takes that away. Third, it gets dark here before 7 pm in late October, and sidewalks full of kids in dark costumes don't see a dog until the last second.

Get the walk in before the doorbell rush starts. Photo: Spare Human Services

So walk before the candy hours start, keep the leash short through busy stretches, and put something reflective or a light-up collar on your dog if you'll be out at all after sunset. It's the same advice I give before Fourth of July fireworks: tire them out early, then give them a quiet place to ride out the noise and doorbell chaos.

Costumes: your dog gets a vote

Some dogs genuinely don't mind a costume. Most dogs tolerate one at best. Try it on a week early, in short sessions, and watch for the signs that say "I'm not okay with this" — tucked tail, frozen posture, lip licking, whale eye. If you get any of those, a bandana or a themed collar counts as a costume. Your dog doesn't know it's Halloween, they just know something is strapped to their back.

A few hard rules: nothing over the eyes or ears, nothing with rubber bands or tight elastic, and nothing the dog can chew off and swallow. And costumes on cats are a hard no — almost every cat I've met would disagree with the plan loudly.

Decorations: flames, wires, and cobwebs

The fix here is simple: battery-operated tea lights instead of real candles in your jack-o'-lanterns. A tipped-over pumpkin with a real flame in it is a fire, not a decoration. Keep decorative wires and light strings out of chewing range, and if you use fake cobwebs, keep them off the floor and away from where a cat can pull them down. Fake cobwebs are exactly the kind of dangly texture a cat can't resist.

Black cats and outdoor cats

Bring cats inside a day early and keep them in a day late — black cats especially, but honestly any cat on a night when the front door opens fifty times and the streets are full of people. A cat that's used to a doggy door will complain. Complaints are better than the alternative.

If you're out on Halloween

Halloween parties, trick-or-treat duty with the kids, or a late shift at work — that's the night your pets are home alone during the most chaotic evening of the year. This is exactly what drop-in visits are for. A quick stop during peak trick-or-treat hours to let the dog out, sweep the yard for dropped candy, and make sure nobody has decided the candy bowl was worth the effort. Book it a week or two out, because the 31st fills up the same way holiday weekends do.

Halloween is a great night around here — the weather finally breaks a little, the neighborhoods come alive, and half the dogs on my route get to dress up as ghosts. With the candy up high, the dog away from the door, and the walk done early, it stays a great night for the pets too.
